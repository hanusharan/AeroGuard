# AeroGuard — Precursor Diagnosis (Phases 1–2)

Read-only analysis of the existing, unmodified v0.2 dataset
(`data/processed/processed_dataset_v2.parquet`, `data/raw/raw_telemetry_v2.parquet`,
`data/metadata/trajectory_metadata_v2.csv`). Nothing in `aeroguard/`, `data/`, or v0.2
was modified to produce this report. Generated by `scripts/diagnose_precursor.py`.

Stall boundary for this aircraft (CL(alpha) peak, numerically located): **16.07 deg**.

## Phase 1 — Is there measurable precursor information at 5/4/3/2/1/0.5s?

Method: for every real stall-crossing trajectory (192 of 1000, 161 from the `stall`
regime, 31 from `near_boundary`), pull the row exactly `{5,4,3,2,1,0.5,0}`s before the
first-stall time. Compare against a "safe" background: one randomly chosen row from
each of the 808 trajectories that never stall (mix of `normal` and non-crossing
`near_boundary`). Effect size = Cohen's d; separability = single-feature AUC
(symmetric, 0.5 = chance).

| Lead time | alpha AUC | stall_margin AUC | V AUC | elevator AUC | dalpha_dt AUC | dV_dt AUC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5s | 0.611 | 0.611 | 0.600 | 0.612 | 0.554 | 0.566 |
| 4s | 0.599 | 0.599 | 0.589 | 0.600 | 0.557 | 0.556 |
| 3s | 0.608 | 0.608 | 0.599 | 0.609 | 0.559 | 0.562 |
| 2s | 0.611 | 0.611 | 0.601 | 0.611 | 0.561 | 0.564 |
| 1s | 0.608 | 0.608 | 0.595 | 0.587 | 0.530 | 0.567 |
| 0.5s | 0.605 | 0.605 | 0.596 | 0.713 | 0.691 | 0.639 |
| 0s (crossing) | 0.724 | 0.724 | 0.610 | 0.723 | 0.728 | 0.744 |

Full table: `precursor_stats.csv`.

**Findings, per lead time:**

- **A. At 5s: weak but non-zero (~0.60–0.61 AUC).** Trajectories that will eventually
  cross already sit slightly nearer the boundary and already have slightly elevated
  elevator even 5s out — a real but weak effect (Cohen's d ≈ 0.28–0.38 for
  alpha/stall_margin/elevator/V).
- **B. At 4s: essentially the same (~0.59–0.60 AUC).**
- **C. At 3s: essentially the same (~0.60–0.61 AUC).**
- **D. At 2s: essentially the same (~0.60–0.61 AUC).**
- **E. At 1s: essentially the same (~0.59–0.61 AUC), elevator/derivative signal not yet
  sharpening.**
- **At 0.5s: elevator and dalpha_dt jump (0.71, 0.69)** — the acute pitch-up is now
  underway.
- **At 0s (the crossing itself): all state/derivative features sharpen (0.61–0.74).**

**The critical pattern is not decay — it's a flat plateau followed by a late step.**
Separability barely changes across the entire 5s→1s range (all within ~0.59–0.61 AUC
band), then jumps only inside the last 0.5s. This independently reproduces the temporal
ML experiment's own physics-diagnosis finding (`outputs/ml_temporal/metrics/physics_diagnosis.csv`:
"0.51–0.61 AUC at every lead time 1–5s"). The 5s-out signal is a **regime-level**
signal (these are already `stall`/`near_boundary`-regime trajectories with a pulse
in flight) — not an **approach-in-progress** signal. There is essentially nothing in
the physics state that tells you "the boundary is 3 seconds away" as opposed to "the
boundary is 5 seconds away" — both look almost identical. Statistical difference
(reliably measurable, non-zero effect size) is present at all lead times; useful
*trend* information that would let a model estimate time-to-crossing is not.

## Confirming timing: how fast is the actual approach?

From the real 192 crossing trajectories (`ramp_timing_stats.json`):

- **Median time for alpha to cross 8deg → 16deg (the boundary): 0.37s** (mean 1.03s,
  IQR 0.30–0.57s; the mean is pulled up by a long tail of `near_boundary` cases with
  slower, partial approaches).
- **Median time from alpha=12deg to the actual crossing: 0.18s** (IQR 0.14–0.25s).

This matches the temporal ML report's own finding (median credited lead time 0.53s,
reliable recall only under 0.5s) and explains it mechanistically: **the informative part
of the transition genuinely only exists for a few tenths of a second** in the current
dataset. Sample trajectory plot: `01_alpha_before_crossing.png`.

## Phase 2 — Physical root cause

Traced real trajectories (e.g. `traj_0247`, `stall` regime, and `traj_0914`,
`near_boundary` regime) directly and confirmed the mechanism with two targeted control
experiments run through the unmodified physics engine (`aeroguard/`, via
`aeroguard_dataset.trajectory_sim.simulate_trajectory` — no physics changed).

**1. Alpha's equilibrium is set almost entirely by elevator, not by V/throttle.**
The pitch model (`aeroguard/dynamics.py`) is
`Iyy*dq/dt = elevator_effectiveness*delta_e - pitch_damping*q - alpha_stiffness*alpha`.
Solving for the q=0 equilibrium: `alpha_eq ≈ (elevator_effectiveness/alpha_stiffness) *
delta_e = 3.2 * delta_e` (radians), independent of V, throttle, or gamma. **Verified
empirically**: cutting throttle from trim to 0 for 19s while holding elevator fixed at
trim moved alpha by under 0.5 deg total (3.93° → 4.41° → back to 3.68°) — the
alpha-restoring term continuously re-trims theta to track the elevator-implied
equilibrium regardless of what gamma/V are doing. **Consequence: only elevator timing
can engineer a genuine slow approach in this model; throttle profiles cannot.**

**2. The natural (short-period) pitch response is fast — ~0.4–0.5s — and is fixed by
aircraft parameters we must not change.** Analytically, from `Iyy=1285`,
`alpha_stiffness=1.0e4`, `pitch_damping=6.0e3`:
`omega_n = sqrt(alpha_stiffness/Iyy) = 2.79 rad/s`, damping ratio
`zeta = pitch_damping / (2*sqrt(alpha_stiffness*Iyy)) = 0.84` (near-critically-damped),
giving an effective settling time of roughly 1.3–1.7s to fully reach a new equilibrium,
but with the **fastest** rate of change concentrated in the first ~0.4–0.5s — which
matches the measured 0.37s median 8→16deg crossing time almost exactly.

**3. The calibrated regimes reach the boundary via short pulses aimed at a distant
equilibrium, so the boundary is crossed during the fastest part of the response, not
the slow tail.** `near_boundary`'s pulse (0.12–0.20 rad, rise 0.4–1.0s) implies
`alpha_eq ≈ 22–37 deg` — well past the 16 deg boundary. `stall`'s pulse (0.19–0.32 rad)
implies `alpha_eq ≈ 40–68 deg`. Because the pitch model's response rate is
proportional to distance-from-equilibrium, aiming far past the boundary produces the
*fastest* alpha rate exactly while passing through the 8–16deg zone — the opposite of
what a gradual approach needs. This is the direct cause of the sub-0.5s transition
measured in Phase 1.

**4. A slow-rise elevator profile targeting an equilibrium only slightly past the
boundary produces a genuinely gradual, multi-second approach.** Tested directly
(unmodified physics, single elevator pulse, no other code changes):

| Elevator profile | Result |
|---|---|
| rise=0.5s, magnitude=0.11 rad (fast, old-style) | alpha 4°→13° in **1.4s** |
| rise=3.0s, magnitude=0.095 rad (slow) | alpha 4°→8°→12° over **~3.0s**, still climbing smoothly at t=6s |
| rise=4.0s, magnitude=0.11 rad (slow) | alpha 4°→8°→12°→15.5° over **~4.6s**, smooth monotonic climb the whole way |

The slow-rise cases track the elevator ramp quasi-statically (forcing timescale, 3–4s,
now dominates over the ~0.4–0.5s natural response), producing exactly the desired
`SAFE → GRADUAL APPROACH → NEAR STALL` shape instead of a snap. This is the mechanism
Phase 3's candidates are built on.

**Bottom line root cause:** the short precursor window in v0.2 is a **control-profile
timing artifact, not a physics limitation**. The validated aircraft dynamics can
produce a multi-second, physically smooth approach to the boundary — the existing
`near_boundary`/`stall` regimes just never asked for one; they use short-rise,
far-past-boundary-equilibrium pulses because that was what v0.1→v0.2 calibration
optimized for (reliable crossing / low gamma-envelope-termination rate — see
`aeroguard_dataset/config.py`'s `NEAR_BOUNDARY_CONTROL_CONFIG` docstring), not for
precursor duration.
